# Philosophy Books (by Branch)
Branches Covered:
- Epistemology
- Metaphysics
- Logic
- Axiology (Aesthetics & Ethics)
- Political Philosophy

Reading strategy:
- Start with **Foundations** (1–3 books per branch).
- Then add **Core** (builds vocabulary + canonical debates).
- Use **Advanced / Contemporary** last (best ROI once you have the map).

## Epistemology

### Foundations (order)
1. Plato — *Theaetetus* (knowledge; “what is knowledge?”)
2. René Descartes — *Meditations on First Philosophy* (skepticism; certainty)
3. David Hume — *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (induction; causation; empiricism)

### Core
- John Locke — *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (ideas; empiricism)
- Immanuel Kant — *Prolegomena* (bridge into Kant’s epistemology)
- Edmund Gettier — “Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?” (paper; modern trigger)
- Laurence BonJour — *The Structure of Empirical Knowledge* (coherentism / foundationalism)

### Advanced / Contemporary
- Alvin Goldman — *Epistemology and Cognition* (reliabilism; naturalized angles)
- Ernest Sosa — *Knowledge in Perspective* (virtue epistemology)
- Timothy Williamson — *Knowledge and Its Limits* (knowledge-first)

## Metaphysics

### Foundations (order)
1. Aristotle — *Metaphysics* (substance; being; causation)
2. G. W. Leibniz — *Discourse on Metaphysics* + *Monadology* (necessity; identity; metaphysical system)
3. W. V. O. Quine — “On What There Is” (ontology; commitments)

### Core
- David Lewis — *On the Plurality of Worlds* (modal realism; modality)
- Peter van Inwagen — *Metaphysics* (clean modern survey + positions)
- E. J. Lowe — *A Survey of Metaphysics* (category ontology)

### Advanced / Contemporary
- David Armstrong — *Universals: An Opinionated Introduction* (realism about universals)
- Jaegwon Kim — *Mind in a Physical World* (mind/body; causation)

## Logic

### Foundations (order)
1. Graham Priest — *Logic: A Very Short Introduction* (what logic is; why it matters)
2. Jon Barwise & John Etchemendy — *Language, Proof and Logic* (hands-on intro; proofs)
3. Herbert Enderton — *A Mathematical Introduction to Logic* (standard “serious” next step)

### Core
- Alfred Tarski — *Introduction to Logic and to the Methodology of Deductive Sciences* (classic clarity)
- Raymond Smullyan — *First-Order Logic* (proof practice; puzzles; rigor)

### Advanced / Contemporary
- Kurt Gödel — *On Formally Undecidable Propositions...* (incompleteness; the turning point)
- George Boolos, John Burgess, Richard Jeffrey — *Computability and Logic* (logic + CS bridge)

## Axiology (Aesthetics & Ethics)

### Ethics — Foundations (order)
1. Plato — *Republic* (justice; the good; moral psychology)
2. Aristotle — *Nicomachean Ethics* (virtue; flourishing)
3. Diogenes Laërtius — *Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers* (primary source-ish access to Cynics incl. Diogenes; better than “lost works”)
4. Immanuel Kant — *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (deontology; autonomy)
5. John Stuart Mill — *Utilitarianism* + *On Liberty* (consequentialism; freedom)

#### Ethics — Schools / figures (classical)
- Diogenes of Sinope (Cynicism): works mostly lost; use *Lives and Opinions* + secondary histories to place him
- Epictetus — *Enchiridion* (Stoicism; practical ethics)
- Epicurus — *Letter to Menoeceus* (hedonism; ataraxia; concise primary)

### Ethics — Core / Modern
- G. E. M. Anscombe — “Modern Moral Philosophy” (virtue ethics revival)
- Philippa Foot — *Virtues and Vices* (virtue ethics; practical reason)
- Bernard Williams — *Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy* (critique; integrity)

### Ethics — Advanced / Contemporary
- Derek Parfit — *Reasons and Persons* (identity; reasons; population ethics)
- T. M. Scanlon — *What We Owe to Each Other* (contractualism)
- Christine Korsgaard — *The Sources of Normativity* (Kantian constructivism)

### Aesthetics — Foundations (order)
1. David Hume — “Of the Standard of Taste” (taste; standards)
2. Immanuel Kant — *Critique of Judgment* (beauty; judgment; sublime)
3. Leo Tolstoy — *What Is Art?* (art; morality; expression)

### Aesthetics — Core / Contemporary
- Arthur Danto — *The Transfiguration of the Commonplace* (artworld; meaning)
- Kendall Walton — “Categories of Art” (perception; genre; interpretation)
- Roger Scruton — *Beauty* (accessible, opinionated survey)

## Political Philosophy

### Foundations (order)
1. Thomas Hobbes — *Leviathan* (state; security; sovereignty)
2. Niccolò Machiavelli — *The Prince* (political realism; power; stability vs virtue)
3. John Locke — *Second Treatise of Government* (rights; consent)
4. Jean-Jacques Rousseau — *The Social Contract* (general will; legitimacy)
5. John Stuart Mill — *On Liberty* (liberty; harm principle)

### Core (20th century anchors)
- Isaiah Berlin — “Two Concepts of Liberty” (negative/positive liberty)
- Hannah Arendt — *The Origins of Totalitarianism* (power; ideology; terror)
- John Rawls — *A Theory of Justice* (justice as fairness)
- Robert Nozick — *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (libertarian reply to Rawls)

#### Political realism (optional mini-track)
- Niccolò Machiavelli — *Discourses on Livy* (republicanism; institutions; complements *The Prince*)
- Max Weber — “Politics as a Vocation” (ethic of responsibility; legitimacy; state)
