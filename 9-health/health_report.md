## What Changed, What Got Worse, What to Improve

### 🔴 Getting worse — needs attention

1. **Weight climbed from 80 kg → 91 kg (BMI 27.68 → 31.12), moving you from "Overweight" into "Obese."** This is an 11 kg gain and it's the most likely common thread behind several of the other findings below — it lines up in time with the rise in liver enzymes, triglycerides/VLDL, and uric acid. This is probably the single highest-leverage thing to address.

2. **Liver enzymes (SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST) are rising steadily and are now clearly abnormal.**
   ALT: 24 → 69 → 89 IU/L. AST: 38 → 57 → 72 IU/L. Both were normal in 2024 and are now roughly **1.5–2x the upper limit** as of Sep 2026, and the trend is upward at every check, not just elevated once. This is the single most concerning trend in your data. Common causes: fatty liver (often linked to weight/lipids), alcohol, certain medications/supplements, or viral hepatitis (your HBsAg is negative, which rules out Hep B). **This should be discussed with a doctor** — likely a liver ultrasound and repeat LFT are warranted.

3. **Uric acid has been high at all three measurements** (7.9 → 7.4 → 7.7 mg/dL, all above the 3.4–7.2 range). This is a persistent pattern, not a one-off. Notably, your **April 2026 kidney ultrasound found a probable small kidney stone** — chronically high uric acid is a classic risk factor for uric-acid kidney stones and gout. These two findings are very likely connected.

4. **Thyroid (TSH) is trending upward into abnormal territory**: 1.91 (normal) → 4.65 (upper-normal) → 5.747 (**above range**). This suggests possible early/subclinical hypothyroidism developing. Worth a repeat thyroid panel with your doctor, especially since it's a clear, consistent upward trend across three separate checks.

5. **Triglycerides and VLDL spiked and remain elevated.** Triglycerides went from a healthy 49 → a very high 291 → normalized to 130 (different lab, possibly not fasted the same way) → back up to 174. VLDL mirrors this (10 → 58 → 26 → 35). The swings suggest this is diet/lifestyle sensitive (alcohol, sugar, fasting status before the blood draw) rather than a fixed condition — but it's been above-normal in 3 of 4 readings.

6. **Persistent relative lymphocytosis**: Lymphocyte % has been above range every single time it was checked (42 → 56 → 52), with neutrophils correspondingly on the low side. Isolated, this is often benign (can reflect a recent minor viral infection or just individual baseline), but the consistency across two years is worth mentioning to a doctor, especially paired with the other findings.

7. **Platelet count is drifting down**, though still technically in range: 218,000 → 175,000 → 150,000 (now sitting exactly at the lower limit). Worth rechecking to make sure it doesn't dip further.

8. **Hemoglobin and hematocrit are gently declining**: Hb 15.0 → 14.1 → 13.7 g/dL; HCT 43.9 → 42.0 → 41.7%. Still within normal range, but a steady downward slope over two years is worth watching (diet/iron intake).
